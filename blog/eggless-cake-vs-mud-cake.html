Eggless Cake vs Mud Cake: What's the Real Difference?

By Tarun Patel 1 August 2026 8 min read Eggless Cakes · Ingredients

"Eggless" and "mud cake" describe two completely different things, and mixing them up leads to the wrong order more often than you'd expect. Eggless is about what's missing from the recipe. Mud cake is a specific texture — dense, fudgy, deeply chocolatey — that has nothing to do with eggs one way or the other. What Is the Difference Between Mud Cake and Regular Sponge Cake?

Mud cake is much denser than a regular sponge, built from real melted chocolate and butter rather than a light, whipped batter. Sponge cake gets its rise from whipping eggs and sugar together before folding in flour, which produces an airy crumb. Mud cake is baked at a lower temperature for longer, producing a tighter, richer, more fudgy result.

That difference in method matters enormously once you take eggs out of the picture. Sponge cake's entire structure depends on trapped air from whipped eggs. Mud cake's structure depends far more on melted chocolate and butter holding everything together. Eggs still play a role, but a much smaller one relative to what's happening chemically in a sponge.

Mud cake is built from real melted chocolate and butter baked at a lower temperature for longer, producing a denser, fudgier crumb than a whipped-egg sponge cake (Dello Mano, What Is Mud Cake?). That structural difference is exactly why mud cake adapts to egg-free baking more readily than a classic sponge.

Can Mud Cake Be Made Eggless, and Does It Taste the Same?

Yes, and it holds up remarkably well. Because mud cake's richness comes primarily from melted chocolate and butter rather than whipped eggs, removing eggs changes far less about the final result than it would in a sponge. The dense, fudgy character survives the substitution almost entirely intact.

[Unique Insight]

Mud cake is genuinely the easier style to make convincingly eggless. A sponge relies on whipped eggs for structure, so an egg-free version needs real reformulation to avoid a flatter, denser crumb. Mud cake's whole appeal is density, which means an egg-free mud cake can taste closer to its traditional counterpart than an egg-free sponge ever does.

That's the exact logic behind our Chocolate flavour, made in a rich, dense style that reads as mud-cake territory rather than a light sponge. Customers who specifically ask for "something like mud cake, but eggless" are usually pointed toward Chocolate first.

Is Eggless Cake the Same as Vegan Cake?

No, and this is one of the most common mix-ups we hear. Eggless only means eggs are absent. Vegan means no animal products at all, including dairy. Every cake at Num Num's Bakery is 100% eggless, but our cakes still contain dairy — milk powder and butter, the same ingredients that give mud cake its richness in the first place. Both eggs and milk are listed among Australia's priority food allergens by Food Standards Australia New Zealand (FSANZ), which is exactly why we're specific about which one our kitchen actually removes.

That distinction matters most for the mud-cake-style Chocolate flavour specifically. Real melted butter is central to how mud cake achieves its dense, fudgy texture, and a genuinely vegan version needs a dairy-free fat substitute as well as an egg substitute. We don't currently offer a dairy-free option, so a vegan customer would need to look elsewhere for a fully plant-based mud cake.

[Personal Experience]

We get this question most often from customers ordering for a mixed guest list — some who avoid eggs, some who avoid dairy. Being upfront about the difference matters more to us than a quick sale. A customer with a dairy allergy needs a straight answer, not a vague assumption that "eggless" covers everything.

For Hindu vegetarian households, Jain households, and anyone avoiding eggs specifically, our full menu works without any adjustment. For guests who are lactose intolerant rather than dairy-free by choice, note that lactose intolerance and a dairy allergy are different things — a lactose-intolerant guest may tolerate small amounts depending on severity, but we'd rather you make that call with full ingredient information in hand than guess.

If you're catering to a mixed group with both egg and dairy restrictions, message us before ordering. We'll walk through exactly what's in your chosen flavour so nobody at the table is caught off guard.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is eggless cake the same as mud cake?

No. Eggless refers to the absence of eggs and works across any cake style, while mud cake is a specific dense, fudgy texture made from melted chocolate and butter. Num Num's Bakery makes eggless versions of chocolate and other rich flavours in that mud-cake-style texture.

Can you make an eggless mud cake?

Yes. Mud cake's dense texture comes primarily from melted chocolate and butter rather than whipped eggs, which makes it genuinely easier to reproduce eggless than an airy sponge. Num Num's Chocolate flavour is made in that rich, dense style, fully egg-free from a dedicated kitchen.

Does eggless cake taste different from regular mud cake?

A well-made eggless mud cake tastes very close to a traditional one, since the rich chocolate and butter flavour comes through regardless of eggs. The difference is more noticeable in a light sponge cake, where eggs play a bigger structural role in the final texture.

Which is better for a birthday: eggless mud cake or eggless sponge?

It depends on preference. Mud-cake-style flavours like Chocolate suit guests who want a rich, dense dessert, while lighter flavours like Vanilla or Red Velvet suit those who prefer an airier bite. All 15 flavours at Num Num's are 100% eggless as standard.
