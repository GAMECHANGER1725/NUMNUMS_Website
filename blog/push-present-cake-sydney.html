Push Present Cakes: Welcoming a New Mum Home in Sydney

By Tarun Patel 24 Aug 2026 10 min read Cake Gifting · New Mum

A push present cake is not the same thing as a baby shower cake. One is ordered while a baby is still on the way, iced with pastel bunting and "oh baby" script, and eaten at a party the mum-to-be spends most of attending to everyone else. The other arrives afterwards — once labour is over, once the baby is actually here — and it is, quite deliberately, a gift for her. Sydney families are catching on to the idea fast, and a growing number are calling Num Num's Bakery to order one in the first week or two after a birth.

If you're trying to work out what size to order, which flavour travels well to a recovery room or a lounge room full of visiting relatives, and whether a standard bakery cake is even appropriate for a mum who might be breastfeeding, this guide walks through all of it. We'll also draw a clear line between this and a baby shower order, since the two get confused constantly and they solve genuinely different problems.

What Is a Push Present Cake, and Why Are Sydney Families Ordering One in 2026?

A push present cake is a small celebration cake ordered specifically to mark the birth itself, given to the mother as a gift rather than shared out at a pre-birth event. The term borrows from "push present" — a piece of jewellery or a gift traditionally given to a mother after labour — and simply applies the same idea to cake. It's a newer request at Num Num's Bakery than birthday or baby shower orders, but it's grown steadily since 2024 as more Sydney families realise the celebration doesn't have to stop once the baby arrives.

Why now? Partly because social media has normalised the idea of celebrating the mother, not just the baby — "meet the baby" gatherings and hospital-room photos of a small welcome cake are common online, and people copy what they see. Partly because more households are spacing visits out over the first fortnight rather than one big shower, which naturally creates more small occasions worth marking with something sweet.

A push present cake is ordered after birth and gifted to the mother, distinct from a baby shower cake ordered beforehand for a group event (Num Num's Bakery order data, 2026). Sydney requests for this style of order have grown steadily since 2024, tracking a broader shift toward celebrating the postpartum period, not just the pregnancy.

When Should You Order a Cake to Welcome a New Mum Home?

Most Sydney customers order between day 3 and day 10 after the birth, once mother and baby are home and the first wave of visitors is starting to arrive — NSW Health notes that the early postpartum period is when rest and support matter most, which is exactly why families time a gift cake to land during that window rather than the birth day itself. A cake ordered too early sits in a fridge; one ordered around the homecoming lands when it will actually get eaten and enjoyed.

Isn't it a bit odd to plan a cake order around someone else's recovery timeline? Not really — it's the same logic as any thoughtful gift. You wouldn't drop off a hot meal the moment someone walks in the door exhausted; you'd time it for when they've had a chance to settle. Cake works the same way.

What Size Cake Works Best for a Hospital Welcome or a Small Family Gathering?

[Original Data]

Over the first seven months of 2026, individual and 6-inch cakes accounted for the majority of Num Num's push present orders, well ahead of 8-inch and tiered sizes. From our own order history, that pattern makes sense: these are quiet, small gatherings, not full parties, so a mountain of leftover cake helps no one.

An individual-size cake (single serve, sometimes called a mini cake) suits a hospital or first-visit gift beautifully — it doesn't need cutting, it travels well in a small box, and it's gone before it gets a chance to sit around. A 6-inch round is the next step up, feeding roughly six to eight people, which covers a small "meet the baby" gathering of grandparents and close family without excess.

Only go larger — 8-inch or above — if you're genuinely planning a bigger get-together, closer to a first-month celebration than a quiet welcome. For guidance on how many people each size actually feeds, the Our Cakes page lists sizing next to every flavour, and our Order Online page has a simple size guide if you're unsure.

Which Flavours Suit a New Mum and Her Visiting Guests?

Lighter, fresher flavours dominate push present orders — from our order history, Vanilla, Strawberry, Blueberry, and Mango together make up a large share of this specific order type, well above their share of birthday cake orders. That's a genuine pattern, not a coincidence: these flavours photograph softly, pair well with pastel decoration, and don't feel too rich for a mum who might still be adjusting her appetite in the early weeks.

[Personal Experience]

Across the new-mum cake orders we've taken this year, the overwhelming majority ask for a single simple design rather than a multi-tier build — a request pattern we rarely see on birthday orders. From taking these orders directly, customers want a soft colour palette and the baby's name piped in one clean line. It's a gift meant to feel gentle, not showy.

Richer flavours like Chocolate, Ferrero Rocher, or Black Forest still work well if that's genuinely her favourite — this is a personal gift, so her taste should win over any general trend. Fruit-forward options like Pineapple and Lychee are worth considering too if you know she prefers something less sweet. Is It Safe to Send a Cake to a Breastfeeding or Recovering New Mum?

Every cake at Num Num's Bakery is made without eggs, which removes one common allergen from the picture entirely — but it is not a medically restricted or allergen-free product overall. Standard cakes still contain dairy (milk powder, butter) and wheat, and some flavours involve nuts, so this is ordinary celebration food, not a clinically tailored one.

That distinction matters more than people expect. A new mum recovering from birth, possibly breastfeeding, may have her own reasons to avoid certain ingredients that have nothing to do with an egg allergy — a lactose sensitivity, a gluten intolerance, a personal dietary choice. Rather than guessing, the simplest approach is to mention any dietary need when you place the order, so we can confirm exactly what's in the flavour you've chosen before it's made.

According to the Australian Bureau of Statistics, first-time mothers in NSW are increasingly having children in their early thirties (ABS, Population statistics), a demographic shift that has coincided with new mums researching food and ingredient choices more carefully than previous generations. Plain, honest labelling matters to this group specifically.

We're not medical professionals, and nothing here should replace guidance from a midwife, GP, or lactation consultant. If in doubt about any ingredient for a recovering or breastfeeding mum, the safest step is always to check with her healthcare provider first, then confirm the specific flavour details with us directly.

How Do You Personalise a Cake Message for a New Baby Without Overdoing It?

[Unique Insight]

From comparing our own order notes side by side, new-mum cake messages run noticeably shorter than birthday cake messages, which tend to carry full names, ages, and in-jokes. New-mum requests do the opposite: the shortest, plainest wording is almost always what people choose — just a first name, or a single word like "Welcome" or "Congratulations." It reads as more sincere than anything elaborate.

A one-line piped message across the top of the cake is the standard approach — the baby's name, a birth date, or a simple greeting. Keep decoration soft: a pastel colour, a small floral or cloud motif, nothing too literal like an oversized stork or nappy graphic, which can feel a bit much on a gift meant for the mother rather than a baby-themed party. For a longer list of message wording ideas across occasions, our cake message ideas guide has plenty of options that translate well to this context.

If you want to say more than a single line allows, put the longer sentiment on a card instead and keep the cake itself simple. That split — short message on the cake, longer note on paper — tends to look more considered than trying to fit everything onto the icing.

How Are New Mum Welcome Cakes Different from Baby Shower Cakes?

The core difference is timing and audience: a baby shower cake is ordered before the birth for a planned party attended mostly by guests, while a push present or new-mum welcome cake is ordered after the birth as a personal gift to the mother, often shared with only a handful of close visitors. Confusing the two leads to the wrong size, the wrong decoration style, and sometimes the wrong occasion entirely.

Baby shower cakes lean into bump photos, gender-reveal colours, and "oh baby" theming, because the party is a group celebration built around anticipation. Push present cakes skip all of that — there's no bump left to celebrate, no gender to reveal, and the guest list is usually much smaller. If you're planning a pre-birth celebration instead, our dedicated baby shower cake guide covers that occasion in full, including shower-specific design ideas this article deliberately leaves out.

Treating the two as interchangeable is the most common mistake families make when ordering — mixing shower theming into a post-birth gift, or vice versa.
