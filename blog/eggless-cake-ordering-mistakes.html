7 Eggless Cake Ordering Mistakes: What Sydney Customers Get Wrong

By Tarun Patel 29 July 2026 9 min read Eggless Cakes · Ingredients

Most eggless cake problems aren't really about the cake. They're about the order. From our years running two Sydney bakeries, the same handful of mistakes account for almost every disappointed customer we've ever had — and every single one is avoidable if you know what to watch for before you message us.

This isn't a list of baking tips. It's a list of ordering mistakes: the practical, logistical slip-ups that turn a simple cake order into a stressful last-minute scramble. None of them are complicated to fix once you know they exist.

We wrote this guide after noticing the same seven issues resurface, month after month, across both Harris Park and Riverstone. Some are timing problems, some are communication problems, and a couple are simple misunderstandings about what "eggless" actually means. Read through once before you place your next order, and you'll likely avoid every one of them.

  • Eggless isn't vegan or dairy-free — our cakes contain milk and butter.

Why Does Ordering Too Late Cause the Most Problems?

Late ordering causes more disappointment than any other mistake we see. Every cake needs a minimum 48 hours notice, since it's baked fresh rather than pulled from a display case. From our order data, Friday afternoon is consistently our busiest booking window for weekend events — which means customers who message on Friday for a Saturday party often find their preferred flavour, size or time slot already gone.

For a custom or tiered design, the gap is bigger still. Decorating takes real time on top of baking, so we recommend 4-5 days notice for anything beyond a simple flavour-and-size order. During Diwali, Christmas, and NSW school holiday periods, push that out further — those weeks book out fastest across both locations.

From our internal booking data across both Sydney locations, Friday afternoon orders for Saturday pickup have the lowest fulfilment rate of any time slot we track. Ordering by Wednesday for a weekend event gives you the best chance of getting your first-choice flavour, size and pickup time.

Why Do Customers Assume Eggless Also Means Vegan or Dairy-Free?

Eggless only means no eggs. It doesn't mean no dairy, and it doesn't mean vegan. Every cake on our menu contains milk powder, butter or cream — standard ingredients in a lacto-vegetarian kitchen, but not compatible with a vegan or dairy-free diet. This mix-up is common enough that we now ask directly during every custom order conversation.

Personal Experience:

We've had customers arrive to collect a cake assuming "eggless" covered a vegan guest at the party, only to discover the dairy content on pickup day. It's an honest mix-up, not a bait-and-switch — but it's also completely preventable. If a vegan guest is coming, tell us when you order, and we'll be upfront about whether we can help.

According to Food Standards Australia New Zealand (FSANZ), both egg and milk are separately listed among Australia's eight priority food allergens. They're distinct dietary categories, and a bakery removing one doesn't automatically remove the other. We're proudly, entirely egg-free — we're simply not a vegan bakery, and we'd rather say that clearly than let a guest find out the hard way.

How Do Sydney Customers Get Cake Sizes Wrong?

Sizing mistakes usually come from counting invitations instead of guests. RSVP rates for casual birthday parties typically run 70-85% of invitations sent, so a party invited to 30 people often needs a cake for 22-25. Order based on the invite list and you'll either run short or end up with an awkward amount of leftover cake.

  • 8-inch round: serves 15-20 guests — the standard choice for a small family gathering.
  • 10-inch round: serves 25-35 guests — the most common size for a birthday party.
  • Two-tier: serves 40-60 guests — for a milestone birthday, engagement or large cultural celebration.
  • Multiple cakes: for events over 60 guests, two separate cakes in different flavours often works better than one very large tier.

Not sure where your event falls? The Order Online page has a full sizing guide, or just message us your guest count directly and we'll recommend the right size before you commit to a flavour.

Why Does Flavour Indecision Delay So Many Orders?

With 15 flavours on the menu, indecision is a genuine bottleneck. Customers often spend days going back and forth on WhatsApp trying to pick between two or three options, which eats into the notice window they need for baking and decoration.

Unique Insight:

One pattern we've noticed: customers who ask us directly for a recommendation based on their guest mix — kids vs adults, cultural background, dietary needs — decide within one message. Customers who try to decide alone often take three or four days. If you're stuck, just ask us; we do this every day and can usually narrow it down fast.

Why Do Custom Design Changes Get Rejected Close to the Pickup Date?

Custom design changes requested on the pickup day itself usually can't happen, because the cake is already baked and decorated by then. From our experience, most last-minute change requests are small — a colour swap, an extra candle, a different message — but even small changes need time if fondant or piped decoration is already set.

The fix is simple: confirm your design brief when you place the order, and flag any changes at least 24 hours ahead. We'll always try to help on the day, but we can't guarantee it once decoration has started.

Original Data:

From our order history, same-day design change requests cluster on Saturday mornings, right before weekend pickups. Customers who confirm their design brief in writing when they order — rather than describing it verbally on WhatsApp — have far fewer last-minute disputes about what was actually agreed.

How Should You Transport an Eggless Cake to Avoid Damage?

Transport mistakes are the most physically obvious ones — a cake that arrives leaning, smudged or partly melted. Keep the box completely flat, ideally on a car floor rather than a seat, since seats aren't level and braking can slide a cake sideways. Run the air conditioning rather than the heater, and never leave a cake in a parked car for more than 15-20 minutes in warm weather.

Buttercream starts softening noticeably above 25°C, and fondant can slump in direct sun through a car window even on a mild day. If your pickup-to-party gap is more than 30 minutes, ask us about keeping the cake in an esky with an ice brick for the drive — it's a small step that prevents most transport damage we see.

According to the NSW Food Authority, perishable food sits in the "temperature danger zone" between 5°C and 60°C, where bacteria can double roughly every 20 minutes. A cream-based cake left in a warm car for an hour isn't just at risk of looking messy — it's genuinely sitting in the same temperature band the NSW Food Authority warns against for any perishable food. A cooler bag or esky for the drive addresses both the cosmetic and food-safety side of the same mistake.

Why Do WhatsApp Order Mix-Ups Happen — and How Do You Prevent Them?

Miscommunication over WhatsApp causes more disputes than any dishonesty on either side. A flavour name gets typo'd, a date gets misread, or a design brief gets described in a way that means something different to the customer than it does to us. Because everything happens over text rather than in person, small ambiguities slide through unnoticed until pickup day.

Personal Experience:

The clearest orders we get are the ones where a customer sends one message covering flavour, size, date, pickup time, and any dietary notes, all in one go. The messiest are the ones spread across ten short messages over three days, where an early detail gets forgotten by the time we confirm. We always send a written confirmation before your pickup — read it carefully, since that confirmation is what we'll actually bake.

Two habits fix almost every miscommunication we see. First, write your order as a single structured message rather than a back-and-forth conversation — flavour, size, date, pickup time, and any dietary notes, all together. Second, always check our written confirmation reply before your event, not after, so there's time to fix a mistake on either side.

Are There Allergen Mistakes Beyond Eggs to Watch For?

Yes — assuming egg-free automatically means allergy-safe for other allergens is a mistake we still see occasionally. Our cakes contain dairy throughout, and some flavours and decorations may involve nuts. If a guest has an allergy beyond eggs, tell us the specific allergen when you order rather than assuming "eggless" covers it.

For genuine peace of mind on a severe allergy, always confirm directly with us rather than relying on a general description of the cake. We'll give you a complete ingredient breakdown for your chosen flavour before you commit, and we'd rather answer an extra question than have a guest react to something we could have flagged in advance.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most common eggless cake ordering mistake?

Ordering too late is the single most common mistake. Every cake needs a minimum 48 hours notice, and custom or tiered designs need 4-5 days. Friday afternoon is our busiest booking window, so Thursday-or-earlier orders have the best chance of your first-choice flavour and time.

Does eggless mean the same thing as vegan?

No — eggless only means no eggs. Our cakes still contain dairy (milk powder, butter, cream), so they are not vegan or dairy-free. If you need a dairy-free cake for a guest, tell us before ordering so we can advise honestly on what we can and can't accommodate.

How do I avoid ordering the wrong cake size?

Count your actual expected guest list, not the invite list — RSVPs typically run 70-85% of invitations sent. An 8-inch round serves 15-20 guests, a 10-inch serves 25-35, and anything larger should be a two-tier design. Message us your guest count and we'll recommend the right size for you.

Can I change my cake flavour after ordering?

Yes, if you tell us early enough — ideally 24 hours before pickup, since some flavours need advance preparation. Changes requested on the pickup day itself often can't be accommodated because the cake is already baked and decorated by that point.

How should I transport an eggless cake home safely?

Keep the cake flat on a non-slip surface — a car footwell works better than a seat — and run the air conditioning rather than the heater. Avoid leaving it in a parked car for more than 15-20 minutes in warm weather, since buttercream softens quickly above 25°C.
