Is Num Num's Bakery Kosher Certified for Hanukkah?

No, and it's important to say that plainly. Num Num's Bakery is 100% egg-free, but egg-free is not the same thing as kosher. We are not a certified kosher kitchen, and we don't claim kosher status on any product. Eggs are one of eight priority food allergens under Food Standards Australia New Zealand (FSANZ) rules. That's the allergen standard our kitchen is built around, a separate framework entirely from kosher dietary law.

Personal Experience.

We've had customers ask directly whether egg-free means kosher, and the honest answer is no. Kosher certification covers ingredient sourcing, equipment, and supervision requirements that go well beyond removing eggs from a recipe. If kosher certification is essential for your Hanukkah celebration, please contact a certified kosher bakery directly rather than assuming our egg-free kitchen meets that standard.

What we do offer is a genuinely reliable option for Hanukkah hosts who want an egg-free dessert without a formal kosher requirement. That might be for a guest with an egg allergy, a household that prefers eggless baking generally, or simply a family who likes the lighter, moist texture our recipes produce.

What Makes a Cake Genuinely Egg-Free, Not Just "No Eggs Added"?

A cake is only reliably egg-free when the whole kitchen is egg-free, not just the recipe. Under the FSANZ allergen framework, eggs sit alongside milk, peanuts, tree nuts, and five other substances as a priority allergen requiring careful handling. A bakery that swaps eggs out of one recipe still risks cross-contamination if it cracks eggs elsewhere. Shared benches, mixers, and trays carry that risk regardless of what the recipe card says.

At Num Num's Bakery, that risk doesn't exist because eggs never enter the building. Every flavour, every base, every filling is formulated without eggs from the ground up, and no egg product is stored, prepared, or handled on-site. That's a structural decision about the kitchen, not a substitution made per order.

We've found that customers asking about Hanukkah cakes are more likely than average to ask a direct follow-up question about cross-contamination. That's true even when the primary reason for ordering isn't an allergy. Our theory: festival hosts feel more responsible for a shared, multi-night dessert table than for a single birthday cake, so they double-check details they might otherwise assume.

That distinction matters most across an eight-night festival, where the same dessert question comes up night after night rather than once. Knowing the answer is always yes, without needing to re-ask each time, is worth more to most Hanukkah hosts than any single flavour choice.
