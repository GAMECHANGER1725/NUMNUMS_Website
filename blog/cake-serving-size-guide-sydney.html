Cake Serving Size Guide: How Many People Does Each Size Feed?

By Tarun Patel 24 July 2026 10 min read Custom Cakes · Birthday

"What size cake do I need?" is the single most common question we get at Num Num's Bakery, ahead of flavour and even ahead of price. Order too small and you'll be cutting slivers for latecomers. Order too big and half a cake goes home in a container nobody asked for. This guide breaks down exactly how many people each standard round cake size feeds, along with tiered, square and sheet options, so you can order with confidence the first time.

Every size below is baked as a 100% eggless cake at Num Num's, across all 15 flavours, so the sizing logic is identical whichever flavour you choose. These figures come from our own order history — real cakes cut for real Sydney parties, not a generic industry chart borrowed from an American-style dessert-portion standard.

If you're planning a birthday, engagement, milestone celebration, or a school or office event and you're not sure where to start, skim the size chart below, then check the notes on tiered cakes, square versus round, and buffer sizing further down. Between them, they cover the sizing questions we get asked most often at both shops.

How Many People Does a Standard Round Cake Feed?

Round cake sizes are measured by diameter, and serving counts assume a standard party-size slice — smaller than a dessert-restaurant portion, generous enough that nobody feels short-changed. Here's how our most-ordered sizes break down.

Cake SizeParty SlicesBest For
6-inch round8–12 servingsSmall family gathering, cutting cake with cupcakes
8-inch round15–20 servingsTypical birthday party, small office event
10-inch round25–35 servingsLarger birthday, engagement, community gathering
Two-tier (8" + 6")35–45 servingsMilestone birthday, anniversary
Three-tier (10" + 8" + 6")60–80 servingsWedding, large community or corporate event

The 8-inch round is our single most-ordered size, which lines up with the average Sydney birthday party guest list. If your event sits right on the border between two sizes, it's almost always better to round up — a slightly larger cake costs less, per guest, than running short.

Why Do Serving Charts From Different Bakeries Disagree?

Serving counts vary between bakeries because there's no single industry standard for what counts as "a slice." A restaurant-style dessert portion is roughly 2 inches wide; a party-style slice, the kind most Sydney hosts actually cut, is closer to 1–1.25 inches. That difference alone can shift a cake's serving count by 30–40%, which is why one bakery's chart might promise 30 servings from a 10-inch cake while another only claims 22.

At Num Num's, the numbers in this guide come from watching how our own cakes actually get cut at real Sydney parties, not from a generic industry formula. Being 100% eggless doesn't change the serving math either — a common misconception is that eggless sponge is denser and therefore "goes further," but a well-made eggless cake has the same volume and crumb structure as its egg-based equivalent, just without the egg.

Height matters more than most people expect. A standard two-layer 8-inch cake and a taller three-layer 8-inch cake are the same diameter but can differ in serving count by 20–30%, because each slice is taller and therefore more filling. If your event runs long and guests are likely to want seconds, a taller build of the same diameter stretches further than the sizing chart alone suggests.

What About Square or Sheet Cakes?

Square and sheet cakes generally yield more servings than a round cake of the same width, because a square shape has no rounded corners lost to trimming. From our kitchen experience, an 8-inch square cake serves roughly 20–24 people compared with 15–20 for an 8-inch round — a meaningful difference if you're feeding a larger office or classroom group on a fixed size.

  • 8-inch square: approximately 20–24 servings.
  • 10-inch square: approximately 30–40 servings.
  • Quarter sheet: approximately 25–30 servings, popular for school and office events.

Square cakes also cut more efficiently for buffet-style serving, since every slice is close to the same size — useful for corporate events or school celebrations where consistency matters more than presentation, and where guests are serving themselves rather than being handed a plated slice.

How Do I Size a Cake for a Big Milestone Event?

Once a guest list climbs past 40, a single-tier cake stops being practical — the diameter needed becomes awkward to bake, transport and cut evenly. This is where tiered cakes take over. A two-tier 8-inch-plus-6-inch build serves 35–45 guests and still looks proportionate on a table, while a three-tier 10-8-6 build stretches to 60–80 servings for genuinely large events.

From our order history, milestone 40th, 50th and 60th birthdays in the north-west and western Sydney corridors most often land on a two-tier build, while wedding and large community-event orders are where we see three-tier requests. Very few customers order beyond three tiers — at that scale, it's usually more practical and cost-effective to combine a smaller showpiece cake with a cupcake tower for the bulk of the guest list.

Tier proportions matter as much as the raw serving count. A well-built two or three-tier cake keeps each layer visually distinct — roughly a 2-inch difference in diameter between tiers reads clearly, while a smaller gap can look unbalanced once the cake is decorated. If you're planning a tiered order for a milestone event, tell us the occasion and rough guest count and we'll suggest proportions that both serve correctly and look right in photos.

Should I Order Extra Cake for Wastage and Late Arrivals?

A small buffer is worth building in, but it doesn't need to be large. From our order data, a 10% buffer above your confirmed guest count comfortably covers second helpings, a few uninvited-but-welcome extras, and slightly uneven cutting on the day. For a 30-guest party, that means sizing for roughly 33, which usually rounds up cleanly to the next standard size anyway.

Where the buffer matters more is at events with an uncertain final headcount — school functions, community gatherings, or parties where RSVPs are informal. In those cases, rounding up to the next full size is safer than trying to calculate an exact number, since a cake can't be resized on the day the way a cupcake order can.

Household size is also worth factoring in when you're estimating from an invite list rather than a headcount. According to the ABS Household and Families Census release, the average NSW household in 2021 held 2.57 people. If your invite list counts households rather than individuals — common for family-heavy celebrations — multiplying by roughly 2.5 per household, rather than assuming one guest per invite, gets you much closer to the real number walking through the door.

For a school or community event where the guest list is genuinely open — parents dropping in and out over an afternoon rather than a fixed sit-down party — sizing generously and leaning on cupcakes or a sheet cake for the bulk of servings, alongside a smaller showpiece cake, tends to work better than trying to size a single large cake to an unpredictable crowd.

Does Cake Size Affect Price, and When Should I Order Around School Holidays?

Yes — cake price scales with size, since a larger diameter needs more batter, more frosting and more decoration time. From our order data, the price step between an 8-inch and 10-inch round is smaller than most customers expect, because most of the labour (levelling, crumb-coating, decorating) is similar regardless of diameter. See the Order Online page for current pricing by size.

This is also why tiered cakes rarely cost double a single-tier equivalent, even though the serving count roughly doubles. A two-tier build shares a single decorating and delivery process, so the per-serve cost usually comes out lower than ordering two separate single-tier cakes for the same total guest count. If your budget is the main constraint rather than the guest count, ask us directly — sometimes a slightly smaller single tier plus a cupcake top-up works out more economical than stepping up to the next full size.

Timing also affects how far ahead you should book. According to the NSW Department of Education 2026 term dates, NSW school holidays fall around early April, late June to mid-July, late September to mid-October, and mid-December. Birthday and celebration cake orders spike in the days immediately before each of these breaks, so if your event falls in that window, book earlier than the standard 48 hours — 4–5 days gives us room to fit larger or tiered orders in comfortably.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many people does a 6-inch cake serve?

A 6-inch round cake typically serves 8–12 people, depending on whether you cut party-size slices or larger dessert servings. It suits a small family gathering, an intimate dinner, or a cutting cake paired with cupcakes for a bigger guest list.

How many people does an 8-inch cake serve?

An 8-inch round cake serves roughly 15–20 people with standard party slices. It's the most-ordered size at Num Num's Bakery, suiting a typical birthday party, small office celebration, or family milestone gathering with a moderate guest list and a few extras.

How many people does a 10-inch cake serve?

A 10-inch round cake serves approximately 25–35 people. It's a common choice for larger birthday parties, engagement gatherings, and community events where guest numbers run into the mid-thirties without needing a second tier or any extra cupcakes on the side.

Should I order a bigger cake than my guest count to be safe?

A small buffer helps — order for roughly 10% more than your confirmed guest count to cover second helpings, late arrivals, and uneven cutting. For an exact guest count, round up to the next standard size rather than down, since a cake can't be resized after baking.
