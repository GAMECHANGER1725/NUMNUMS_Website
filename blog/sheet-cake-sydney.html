What Is a Sheet Cake, and Why Choose One Over a Tiered Cake?

A sheet cake is a single flat, rectangular cake baked in one large pan, rather than multiple round layers stacked into tiers. It's the most efficient format for feeding a large group, since a single sheet delivers far more servings per dollar than an equivalent tiered design, without the structural engineering a multi-tier cake needs.

Tiered cakes make sense when the cake itself is the visual centrepiece of a formal event, like a wedding. Sheet cakes make sense when the priority is feeding everyone in the room efficiently, which is exactly why they dominate corporate events, school celebrations, and large family gatherings across Sydney.

What Sizes Do Sheet Cakes Come In, and How Many Guests Do They Serve?

Sheet cakes are sold in standard fractional sizes — quarter, half, and full sheet — each scaling servings roughly in proportion to pan size. Using standard 5cm by 5cm party-size slices, a quarter sheet serves 20-25 guests, a half sheet serves 40-50, and a full sheet serves 80-100.

Is a Sheet Cake Easier to Make Eggless Than a Tiered Cake?

Generally, yes. A sheet cake bakes as a single flat layer with no vertical stacking weight to support, which removes the structural risk that comes with an eggless sponge holding up multiple tiers under their own weight. There's no dowelling, no cake boards between layers, and no risk of a top tier compressing a bottom one.

[Personal Experience]

From our experience baking hundreds of sheet cakes, the single biggest risk isn't the eggless recipe at all — it's an unevenly heated oven producing an uneven bake across a large flat surface. We manage that with careful pan rotation and consistent batch sizing, which matters more for a full sheet than it ever does for a smaller round cake.

That said, a large sheet cake still needs careful handling once it's out of the oven. A bigger surface area means more exposed cake and a higher risk of drying out if it isn't stored and transported properly, so we recommend collecting as close to your event start time as your schedule allows.

How Do You Store and Transport a Large Sheet Cake?

A full sheet cake is heavier and more awkward to carry than a round cake, so a flat, stable surface in your vehicle matters more than the box itself. Lay the cake flat in the boot or on a flat back seat rather than propping it upright, and avoid sudden braking on the drive.

Once you're at the venue, keep the cake in a cool spot away from direct sun or heater vents until it's time to cut it. Buttercream softens quickly in Sydney's summer heat, and a large flat surface loses its shape faster than a smaller cake once the icing starts to slide. If your event runs more than two hours after collection, ask us about a slightly firmer finish that holds up better over a longer service window.

What Designs and Flavours Work Best for a Sheet Cake?

From our order data, Chocolate and Vanilla dominate sheet cake orders, largely because they're the safest crowd-pleasers for a mixed group where individual flavour preferences are unknown. Two-flavour split sheets — Chocolate on one half, Vanilla on the other — are a popular way to cover more ground without ordering two separate cakes. Three-way splits are also possible on a full sheet, though we'd recommend keeping designs simple across the join lines.

Are Sheet Cakes Suitable for Dietary and Allergen Needs?

Egg-free baking aligns with several dietary frameworks common at the large group events sheet cakes typically serve. According to Food Standards Australia New Zealand (FSANZ), eggs are one of the eight priority food allergens in Australia. A large office or school event is exactly the setting where at least one guest is likely to be managing an egg allergy.

At Num Num's Bakery, eggs are not used in any product, any preparation area, or any equipment on-site — the entire kitchen is egg-free, not just the recipes. Our cakes still contain dairy — milk powder and butter — and may involve nuts, so we're not an allergen-free bakery. If your group has broader dietary needs beyond eggs, message us before ordering and we'll walk through exactly what's in your chosen flavour. For severe or anaphylactic egg allergy, state your requirements clearly when ordering and follow your own medical advice.
