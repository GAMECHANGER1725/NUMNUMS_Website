Cake for University Events Sydney — O-Week to Graduation, Fully Eggless

By Tarun Patel 9 August 2026 10 min read Custom Cakes

Sydney's university calendar runs on cake more than most students realise. O-Week stalls, society cultural nights, exam-block treat runs, graduation afternoon teas, and the birthday that always lands mid-semester all need one. Students at the University of Sydney, UNSW, Macquarie, UTS and Western Sydney University's Parramatta City campus come from an enormous range of dietary backgrounds. A cake that quietly excludes half the room isn't a small thing at a 100-person club event.

Num Num's Bakery bakes every cake 100% eggless. That covers all 15 flavours and every size, from a single 6-inch birthday cake to a bulk sheet cake for a 200-person O-Week stall. It removes the egg question fully for Hindu vegetarian, Sikh, and many other dietary frameworks common on Sydney campuses. Two Sydney pickup locations, WhatsApp ordering, and 48-hour standard notice make it simple for a busy student committee to organise.

Why Do Sydney's University Events Need Eggless Cakes?

In 2024, Blacktown City Council reported that more than 44% of local residents were born overseas. That figure spans 180+ birthplaces and 185+ languages (Blacktown City Council, 2024 Social Profile, drawing on the 2021 Census). That's the catchment our Riverstone shop sits inside. It's a fair proxy for how mixed a typical Sydney lecture theatre or student society committee actually is.

Dietary practice tracks that diversity closely. Hindu lacto-vegetarian tradition excludes eggs. Many Sikh students follow dietary codes that also exclude eggs. Jain students exclude eggs fully, and a growing number of Muslim students prefer to avoid uncertain ingredient sourcing around eggs and gelatine in shared catering. Add students managing a genuine egg allergy, and a "just get whatever's at the supermarket" cake for a 60-person event leaves several people out on average.

[Unique Insight] A student society committee rarely has one dietary profile to plan around — it usually has four or five overlapping ones in the same room. Ordering from a bakery where eggless is the default, not a special request, collapses that planning problem into a single decision: pick a flavour, not a workaround.

For committee treasurers working with a tight events budget, this also solves a practical problem. Ordering one eggless cake that everyone can eat costs less time and less money than ordering two separate cakes to cover a "regular" and a "dietary" option. What Cake Sizes Work for O-Week Stalls and Campus Events?

O-Week stalls and large club events usually need bulk sheet or slab cakes rather than a single tiered centrepiece. It's cut into small squares so a stall volunteer can hand out slices quickly to passing students. From our internal order data across 2025–26, societies running an O-Week stall most commonly order a bulk sheet cake sized for 80 to 150 students. It's cut into finger-sized portions.

For orders over 100 servings, we usually split the order into two or three sheet cakes across different flavours rather than one giant slab. It's easier to transport to campus, easier to portion at the stall, and it gives passing students an actual choice. Talk to us on WhatsApp about your expected foot traffic and we'll recommend a split.

Do University Clubs and Societies Need Egg-Free Cakes for Cultural Celebrations?

Yes — cultural and religious society events are one of the most consistent sources of eggless cake demand on campus. That's based on what we see across dozens of Sydney student-society orders each year. South Asian Societies, Malayali and Kerala student associations, Islamic Societies, and Chinese Students and Scholars Associations regularly host cultural nights. Egg-free catering directly serves their membership's dietary norms at these events.

For committees catering a Diwali night, an Eid celebration, or a Lunar New Year event, an eggless-by-default bakery removes a genuine planning headache. Hindu-vegetarian members don't need a separate cake. Sikh members following dietary codes that exclude eggs are covered without a special request. Muslim members who prefer to avoid uncertain animal-derived ingredients also have one less thing to check. It's worth being direct here, though: Num Num's is not a certified halal kitchen. Halal-conscious societies should confirm sourcing details with us on WhatsApp before a religious-event order, rather than assume egg-free automatically means halal-certified.

The Food Standards Australia New Zealand (FSANZ) allergen labelling rules apply to bulk campus catering the same way they apply to any food service. Eggs are a priority declarable allergen, and a truly egg-free kitchen removes that specific cross-contamination risk from the room. It doesn't remove dairy or nut risk, though. Committees catering for members with broader allergies should still ask us directly before ordering.

How Does Exam Block Change Cake Orders at Sydney Universities?

[Personal Experience] From our order data across June and November exam periods, WhatsApp order patterns shift. Large event cakes give way to smaller, personal "treat yourself" cakes and study-group celebration cakes ordered right after a final exam. That's a different pattern to the pre-exam HSC results-day rush we see in high school-aged orders each December.

University exam block runs on a different rhythm to school exam season. It drives a distinct order pattern that most bakeries don't plan for. Rather than a single spike around results day, university demand spreads across two windows: the June semester-one finals block and the November semester-two block. Orders cluster in the days right after exams finish, not before.

For study groups planning a post-exam catch-up, a single 8-inch round split across two flavours is the most common order in our data. It's small enough to organise on short notice and works well for a group of 10 to 15 that's just finished a shared unit. Give us the standard 48 hours where possible — exam-block weeks get busy, and a few extra hours of notice makes scheduling easier on our end.

How Do Graduation Afternoon Teas and College Formals Work?

Graduation ceremonies across Sydney's universities usually run in April and September. Family afternoon teas held either on campus grounds or nearby are a well-established tradition around these dates. From our order data, a 10-inch or 12-inch round in Red Velvet or Ferrero Rocher is the most requested combination. It usually serves a graduation tea of 25 to 40 guests, and both flavours photograph well.

Residential college formals follow a different brief again. Committees usually want a single elegant centrepiece cake rather than a bulk sheet, ordered for a sit-down dinner. Custom messages like "Congratulations Class of 2026" or a college crest replicated in fondant are common requests. Message us at least 5 to 7 days ahead for a formal-dinner cake so we can plan any custom decoration properly.

Our catering guide for larger Sydney events covers extra planning detail for milestone graduation gifts. It applies just as well to a graduation afternoon tea as it does to a family party.

Which Flavours Work Best for International Student Welcome Events?

From our order history at welcome events run by international student associations, Chocolate and Vanilla remain the safest crowd-pleasers. They work well when the room includes students who haven't tried Australian-style celebration cake before — familiar, mild, and rarely disliked. For rooms with strong South Asian representation, Rasmalai and Butterscotch consistently outperform other options. Both offer a flavour many attendees recognise from home.

Mango is worth a mention too. It reads as fresh and celebratory without being overly sweet, and works well as a second flavour alongside Chocolate on a split order. If your event has a strict dietary brief beyond eggless — halal-conscious, low-dairy, or otherwise — message us on WhatsApp before ordering. We'll talk through what's achievable for that flavour.
