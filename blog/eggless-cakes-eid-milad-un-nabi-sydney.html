What Is Eid Milad-un-Nabi and How Is It Different from the Two Eids?

Eid Milad-un-Nabi, also written Mawlid al-Nabi or simply Milad, commemorates the birth of the Prophet Muhammad on 12 Rabi' al-Awwal, the third month of the Islamic lunar calendar. Because the Islamic calendar is lunar, the Gregorian date shifts each year — in 2026, it falls on Tuesday, 25 August.

It is entirely separate from Eid al-Fitr, which marks the end of Ramadan, and Eid al-Adha, which falls during the Hajj pilgrimage season. Observance of Mawlid also varies significantly between different schools of Islamic thought. Many Sunni communities, particularly those with South Asian, Middle Eastern, and North African heritage, mark it with gatherings, recitations, and shared meals. Other traditions, including some Salafi and Wahhabi interpretations, do not observe it at all, according to The Conversation's analysis of global Mawlid practice.

South Asian Hindu Festivals — Onam, Durga Puja, Teej and More

Fourteen of the observances on this page come out of India, Nepal and the wider Indian diaspora, and they share a thread that matters here: most are marked with sattvik or strictly vegetarian eating on the day, and in a large share of households that specifically rules out egg. An eggless cake isn't a substitution at these tables. It's the only cake that belongs on them.

Onam

Aug – Sep · Malayalam calendar

Who marks it here: Kerala's Malayali community, whose associations run Onam Sadhya lunches in hired halls through Parramatta, Blacktown and the Hills across late August and September. The festival runs ten days and peaks on Thiruvonam, fixed by the Malayalam calendar and the Thiruvonam star, so the Gregorian date shifts every year.

What it means for your cake: the sadhya is a strictly vegetarian banquet served on a banana leaf and closed with payasam. A cake sits alongside that, not in place of it, so keep it eggless and keep it light. Mango, Lychee and Vanilla all read correctly; heavy chocolate after a sadhya does not. For design, ask for a pookalam-style circular flower arrangement in yellow, orange and white, or the cream-and-gold kasavu border from a Kerala sari. Hall events usually start at 10-inch.

Durga Puja

Sep – Oct · lunar (Ashwin)

Who marks it here: Sydney's Bengali community. Several associations run full pandal pujas in hired halls over the five main days, closing with Vijaya Dashami and Sindoor Khela.

What it means for your cake: bhog is vegetarian, and plenty of families keep egg, onion and garlic out of the kitchen entirely across the puja days. The cake belongs to the Bijoya socials and family dinners that follow, where "Shubho Bijoya" lettering is the standard request. Red and white — the Bengali sari palette — with alpona-style white piping is the design people describe most often. Rasmalai is the closest flavour we bake to the sandesh and mishti doi register these gatherings expect.

Teej

Jul – Sep · lunar

Who marks it here: North Indian and Nepali women's gatherings. Hariyali Teej, Kajari Teej and Hartalika Teej fall across the months of Shravan and Bhadrapada. In Sydney it usually takes the shape of a women's afternoon — green outfits, mehndi, singing, a shared meal.

What it means for your cake: many participants fast, and Hartalika Teej is often kept without food or water until evening, so the cake gets cut when the fast breaks. That timing makes eggless non-negotiable in most households, because the fast is broken with sattvik food. Green and gold with mehndi or paisley piping is the brief we get almost every time. Mango and Rasmalai are the two flavours that suit it.

Maha Shivratri

Feb – Mar · lunar (Phalguna)

Who marks it here: Hindu households across Sydney and the city's Shiva temples, with an all-night vigil, fasting and abhishekam through the night.

What it means for your cake: the fast is the day. Many observers eat no grains at all, so a cake is for the parana meal the next morning or for a community gathering held on the nearest weekend — not for the night itself. Getting that timing wrong is the most common mistake we see on this one. White, silver and deep blue with a crescent moon or trishul motif is the usual design. Fruit-forward flavours like Strawberry, Blueberry and Pineapple sit far better after a fast than anything dense.

Ganesh Chaturthi

Aug – Sep · lunar (Bhadrapada)

Ten days for Ganesha, opened with sthapana at home or at a community venue and closed with visarjan. Modak is the offering; the cake turns up at the welcome gathering or the farewell. One practical note worth having: a lot of families specifically don't want a deity's face on something that gets cut, and ask instead for a modak shape, a lotus, or red-and-gold piping. Vegetarian and eggless throughout the festival days.

Janmashtami

Aug – Sep · lunar (Bhadrapada)

Krishna's birth, marked with a fast that runs to midnight and a celebration that starts the moment it ends. Cakes get cut after midnight or at the following day's gathering. Blue and yellow, a peacock feather, a flute, or a decorated jhulan cradle are the motifs families ask for. Fasting households eat phalahar food, so eggless is the baseline — Rasmalai's kheer-adjacent profile is the closest match on our menu.

Chhath Puja

Oct – Nov · six days after Diwali

Four days of Sun worship kept by Bihari, Jharkhandi and Nepali Terai families, including a nirjala fast with no food and no water. Be clear on this one: thekua and the rest of the prasad are made at home in a purified kitchen, and a bought cake has no place in the ritual itself. The cake is for after parana, when the fast breaks, or for a community get-together. Sun, river-ghat and terracotta-lamp motifs. Many households keep the whole kitchen eggless across the four days.

Pongal

Mid-January (usually 14–17)

Tamil harvest thanksgiving over four days. Sakkarai pongal — new rice, jaggery and ghee — is the dish the festival is named for, which makes Butterscotch the nearest thing we bake to the flavour people are actually chasing. Overflowing pongal-pot imagery, sugarcane and kolam patterns are the design asks. Vegetarian and eggless throughout, and it lands in the same fortnight as Lohri, so mid-January gets busy.

Thaipusam

Jan – Feb · Tamil month of Thai, full moon

A Murugan festival with serious preparation behind it: devotees carrying kavadi commonly fast for days or weeks beforehand on a strict vegetarian, often vegan, diet. The cake is for the meal afterwards, not the vrat. Yellow, orange and saffron with a vel or peacock motif. Worth saying plainly — our cakes are egg-free but they do contain dairy, so they don't suit a strict vegan fasting period.

Bhai Dooj

Oct – Nov · two days after Diwali

The sister-and-brother day that closes the Diwali week: tilak, a shared meal, a gift. In Sydney households a small cake now sits on the same plate as the mithai more often than not, and a 6-inch round is usually the right size. The design briefs overlap almost completely with our Raksha Bandhan cake guide, so start there for ideas.

Basant Panchami / Saraswati Puja

Late Jan – Feb · lunar (Magha)

Saraswati's day, and the day many children are given their first letters in the haate khori ceremony. Yellow runs through everything — clothes, flowers, food — so a saffron or mango-yellow cake with a book, a veena or a swan motif is exactly on brief. Bengali and North Indian households mark it most strongly in Sydney. Mango is the obvious flavour, and the cakes tend to be small and family-sized.

Ugadi

Late Mar – Apr · lunar (Chaitra)

Telugu and Kannada new year, marked with ugadi pachadi — six tastes in one dish, standing for the mix the year will bring. Neem and raw jaggery don't translate into a cake, so families default to Mango or Butterscotch. Mango-leaf toran and kalash motifs. The same window carries Gudi Padwa for Maharashtrian families and Cheti Chand for Sindhi families, which is why late March fills up quickly.

Vishu

14 or 15 April

Kerala's astronomical new year. The Vishukkani is arranged the night before so it's the first thing seen on waking — konna blossom, rice, gold, a mirror — and children are given Vishukkaineettam. Golden yellow is the colour of the day, which makes Mango the natural flavour and konna-flower piping the natural design. Usually a family-sized cake rather than a hall cake.

Lohri

13 January · fixed date

Punjabi midwinter and harvest festival, kept the night before Maghi around a bonfire with rewri, gajak, popcorn and sugarcane. One of the few fixed dates on this page. The order we see most is a first-Lohri cake for a newborn or a newly married couple — a genuine occasion in Punjabi families, not something a bakery invented. Bonfire, dhol and phulkari-embroidery motifs; Butterscotch suits the jaggery register.

Sikh and Nepali Observances

Langar is vegetarian by definition, and Nepali festival kitchens usually are too on puja days. Cakes for these three sit outside the religious programme itself — they get cut at home or at the community hall event that follows.

Guru Nanak Gurpurab

November · full moon of Kartik

The birth anniversary of Guru Nanak, the first Sikh Guru, and the largest date in Sydney's Sikh calendar. Gurdwaras run an Akhand Path over the preceding days, followed by kirtan and langar. Cake isn't part of the gurdwara programme; it's cut at home or at a community hall event afterwards. Khanda motif, saffron-orange and navy, and eggless as a matter of course given langar norms.

Vaisakhi

13 or 14 April

Punjabi harvest festival and the anniversary of the founding of the Khalsa in 1699. Sydney marks it with nagar kirtan processions and gurdwara programmes. Same design language as Gurpurab — Khanda, saffron and navy — with wheat-sheaf and bhangra imagery for the harvest side. It falls in the same crowded mid-April week as Vishu, Songkran and Sinhala & Tamil New Year, so order well ahead.

Dashain & Tihar

Sep – Nov · lunar

Nepal's two largest festivals, a fortnight or so apart — Dashain in Ashwin, Tihar following in Kartik. Nepali families are one of the faster-growing migrant communities in Sydney's west, and Bhai Tika on the final day of Tihar is where a cake most often appears now, beside the sel roti and sweets. Marigold garlands and the seven-colour tika are the design cues. Many families eat vegetarian across the puja days.

Buddhist, Sri Lankan and Thai Celebrations

Vesak Day

May · full moon

Who marks it here: the single day that marks the Buddha's birth, enlightenment and passing. Sri Lankan, Burmese, Thai, Vietnamese, Cambodian and Chinese Buddhist communities across Sydney observe it with temple lantern displays, dansal food offerings and alms-giving. Different traditions fix it to different full moons, so check the date with your own temple rather than assuming.

What it means for your cake: many observers take on additional precepts for the day and eat vegetarian, which is why Vesak orders almost always arrive already specified as eggless before we mention it. Keep the design restrained — white and pale gold, a lotus, a Bodhi leaf, or a simple lantern. Vanilla, Lychee and White Forest suit the day far better than anything dark or rich. For a temple dansal, sheet-style sizing travels and portions better than tiers.

Sinhala & Tamil New Year

13 – 14 April · times set by astrology

Aluth Avurudda for Sinhalese families and Puthandu for Sri Lankan Tamil families, both landing on the same mid-April days, with the auspicious times calculated fresh each year. Sydney's Sri Lankan community runs through Homebush, Strathfield, Wentworthville and Toongabbie. Kiribath, kavum and kokis are the traditional table and a cake joins them rather than replacing them — often in the bright layered style of a Sri Lankan ribbon cake. Butterscotch, Vanilla and Lychee all work; brass oil lamp and sun motifs.

Songkran (Thai New Year)

13 – 15 April

Thailand's new year, marked in Sydney's Thai community with temple merit-making and water-pouring blessings, and echoed by Lao Pi Mai and Cambodian Choul Chnam Thmey across the same days. Mango is the flavour that needs no explanation here. Water-splash motifs, jasmine, and blue-and-gold. Merit-making at the temple usually calls for vegetarian food, so an eggless cake fits the day without a conversation.

East Asian Harvest and Moon Festivals

Mid-Autumn Festival

Sep – early Oct · 15th day, 8th lunar month

Chinese, Vietnamese (Tết Trung Thu) and Malaysian-Chinese families across Hurstville, Chatswood, Eastwood and Cabramatta. A round cake is the whole point — it echoes the full moon. Here's the detail that matters at an eggless bakery: traditional mooncakes very often contain salted egg yolk, so an egg-free cake is a genuine alternative for a household with someone who can't eat it. Lychee is the closest flavour we bake to the lotus-and-red-bean register. Moon, rabbit and lantern motifs; gift-box sizes are common.

Chuseok

Sep – early Oct · same lunar date

Korean harvest thanksgiving — three days of family travel, charye and songpyeon, falling on the same lunar date as the Mid-Autumn Festival. Sydney's Korean community centres on Strathfield, Eastwood, Campsie and Lidcombe. Cakes here are usually gifts carried to a family gathering, so keep the design restrained and make sure it boxes well for the drive. Chestnut and sweet potato are the traditional autumn flavours; Butterscotch and Vanilla are the nearest matches we make.

Middle Eastern, Jewish and Assyrian Observances

Two things to say before this group. Num Num's holds no kosher certification and no kosher supervision, so a cake from us suits a family that doesn't require it. And several observances here involve fasts that exclude dairy as well as eggs — our cakes are egg-free but they do contain dairy, so they belong to the feast, not the fast.

Nowruz

Around 20 – 21 March · the equinox

Persian new year, marked by Iranian, Afghan, Kurdish, Azerbaijani and Tajik families in Sydney along with the Baháʼí community. It's pinned to the vernal equinox in the northern hemisphere, which means in Sydney it arrives at the start of autumn — the one festival on this page that lands in the opposite season to the one it was built for. The haft-sin table sets the palette: green sabzeh, gold, goldfish. Rosewater, saffron and pistachio are the flavour world, and Rasmalai is the closest thing we bake to it.

Rosh Hashanah

Sep – early Oct · 1 – 2 Tishrei

The Jewish new year, marked in Sydney's eastern suburbs and parts of the north shore. Apples dipped in honey, honey cake and a round challah are the signature foods, and round is the shape — the year coming full circle. Honey, apple and pomegranate motifs in gold and deep red. We aren't kosher-certified, so this is a cake for a household that doesn't require kosher supervision; if you do require it, order from a certified bakery.

Purim

Feb – Mar · 14 Adar

Costumes, the Megillah reading, and mishloach manot — parcels of ready-to-eat food sent to friends. Hamantaschen are the traditional pastry. A cake fits the Purim seudah, the festive meal, or works as the centrepiece of a parcel. Bright and playful suits the day far better than formal. The same kosher caveat as Rosh Hashanah applies.

Passover (Pesach)

Mar – Apr · 15 – 22 Nisan

The straight answer, because anyone searching for a Passover cake in Sydney deserves one: a standard Num Num's cake is not suitable for Passover. Chametz — leavened wheat, barley, rye, oats and spelt — is prohibited for the eight days, and ours are wheat-flour cakes with a raising agent. Kosher-for-Passover baking uses matzo meal or potato starch and leans heavily on egg for lift, which is the one ingredient we don't work with, and it needs certified supervision we don't hold. We're glad to bake for a gathering before or after the eight days. During them, go to a certified kosher-for-Passover bakery.

Assyrian New Year (Kha b-Nisan)

1 April · fixed date

Sydney holds one of the largest Assyrian communities outside the Middle East, concentrated around Fairfield, and Kha b-Nisan is its biggest day of the year — traditional dress, parades and community festivals on the first of April. Red, white, blue and gold with the Assyrian star are the design cues, and we'll copy Syriac lettering if you send us the exact wording. One timing note: 1 April can fall inside the Great Fast in some years, and for families keeping it strictly that rules out dairy, so check your church's calendar before ordering.

Orthodox Christmas

7 January

Kept on 7 January by churches following the Julian calendar — Serbian, Macedonian, Russian, Coptic, Ethiopian and Georgian congregations among Sydney's largest. The Nativity Fast that precedes it excludes meat, dairy and eggs for observant families, so our cakes belong to the feast day itself and the days after, not to the fast. Gold, deep red and evergreen, with an Orthodox cross or a wheat motif. Serbian households usually want it alongside česnica rather than instead of it. Our Christmas cake guide covers the 25 December side.

School and Community Dates

Teachers' Day

5 Sep (India) · last Friday in Oct (Australia)

Two different dates in the same city. Indian and Nepali families mark Teachers' Day on 5 September, while Australian schools mark World Teachers' Day on the last Friday in October rather than the global 5 October date. Either way the order has the same shape: one cake for a whole staffroom, or a class cake nobody has to check the label on. Eggless covers the egg-allergic child at the party and the vegetarian teacher in the same slice. Which Festival Cakes Have Dietary Rules We Can't Meet?

Four situations on this page call for something we don't make, and it's better to say so than to take the order. Eggs are one of the priority allergens Food Standards Australia New Zealand requires to be declared on labels — but allergen labelling and religious dietary law are two different things, and it's the second one that decides these four.

  • Passover. Our cakes are chametz. Not suitable, at any size or flavour. A certified kosher-for-Passover bakery is the answer.
  • Anything requiring kosher certification — including Rosh Hashanah and Purim for observant households. We hold none.
  • Strict vegan fasts. The kavadi preparation before Thaipusam, the Orthodox Nativity Fast, and some Ekadashi and Navratri practices exclude dairy. Our cakes are 100% egg-free but contain milk powder and butter.
  • Ritual prasad and naivedyam. At Chhath Puja and most home pujas, the offering is prepared at home under specific purity rules. A bought cake is for the celebration around the ritual, never the offering inside it.

Everything else on this page we can do, and the eggless part is already handled — there's no separate menu, no surcharge, and nothing to request. If you're unsure where your observance sits, message us and we'll tell you plainly rather than guess. For the broader picture of what eggless does and doesn't mean, see eggless versus vegan cakes.

What is Eid Milad-un-Nabi and when is it in 2026?

Eid Milad-un-Nabi, also called Mawlid al-Nabi, marks the birth of the Prophet Muhammad on 12 Rabi' al-Awwal in the Islamic calendar. In 2026 it falls on Tuesday, 25 August. It is separate from Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha, and observance varies between different Islamic traditions.

Is a cake traditional for Mawlid celebrations?

Yes — in many South Asian and Middle Eastern Muslim communities, a Milad gathering closes with a cake cut and shared among guests, alongside sweets and a communal meal. The custom varies by family and cultural background rather than being a fixed religious requirement.

Which flavours suit a Milad gathering?

Rasmalai and Mango suit the South Asian palates common at Milad gatherings, while Chocolate and Vanilla remain reliable choices for mixed-age guest lists. White Forest and Ferrero Rocher work well for adult-heavy community gatherings. All 15 flavours are 100% eggless as standard.

Do you make eggless cakes for Onam, Pongal and other Hindu festivals?

Yes. Every cake we bake is 100% eggless, which is what most Hindu festival kitchens want on a puja day. Onam, Pongal, Ugadi, Vishu, Janmashtami, Ganesh Chaturthi, Teej and Maha Shivratri orders all come through the same WhatsApp line. Tell us the festival and we will match the colours and the flavour.

Can I order a cake for Vesak Day or Durga Puja in Sydney?

Yes. Vesak orders usually want a restrained design — white and pale gold, a lotus or a Bodhi leaf — with Vanilla or Lychee. Durga Puja cakes are typically red and white with Shubho Bijoya lettering, and Rasmalai is the closest flavour we bake to the sandesh register. Both are eggless as standard.

Are your cakes suitable for Passover or a kosher celebration?

No. We hold no kosher certification, and our cakes are wheat-flour cakes with a raising agent, so they are chametz and not suitable for Passover. For Rosh Hashanah or Purim we can bake for a household that does not require kosher supervision. If you do require it, order from a certified kosher bakery.

Which festival dates need more than 48 hours notice?

Mid-April is the tightest week of our year — Vaisakhi, Vishu, Sinhala and Tamil New Year and Songkran all fall within about 72 hours. The September-to-November run of Onam, Durga Puja, Dashain, Chhath Puja and Guru Nanak Gurpurab is the other pressure point. 48 hours is the minimum; allow a week in those windows.

Are your cakes vegan as well as eggless?

No. All 15 flavours are 100% eggless, but they contain dairy — milk powder and butter — so they do not suit a strict vegan fast such as the kavadi preparation before Thaipusam or the Orthodox Nativity Fast before 7 January Christmas.
