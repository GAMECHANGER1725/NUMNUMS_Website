Why Do School Fetes Need Reliable Cake Suppliers?

A cake stall's biggest risk isn't demand, it's supply. Every P&C volunteer has a story about a fete morning with two fewer cakes than the roster promised, because a home baker got sick, ran out of time, or simply forgot. According to the P&C Federation of NSW, nearly 2,000 associations run fundraisers like this every year, almost entirely on volunteer labour.

A bulk order from a dedicated bakery doesn't replace the home-baked charm of a fete, but it does put a floor under supply. Even a partial order — 10 or 15 cakes to backstop the volunteer roster — takes the single biggest failure point off the table.

The stakes are higher than a single disappointing stall. When a P&C's annual budget depends on a handful of major fundraising events, a cake stall that runs out of stock by 10 am isn't just a missed opportunity for families — it's lost revenue that has to be made up somewhere else in the calendar. Treating supply reliability as seriously as the fete's headline attractions is worth the planning effort.

Should a P&C Bake or Buy for a School Cake Stall?

Both approaches work, and most successful stalls actually blend them. Home baking keeps costs low and gives the stall its community character, while a bulk order guarantees a minimum stock level regardless of how many volunteers follow through.

The trade-off usually comes down to what the committee is optimising for. A stall run entirely on volunteer baking maximises profit margin per cake but carries real risk if the roster falls through close to the date. A stall backed by a bulk order sacrifices some margin for certainty, which matters more for a fete the school has spent months promoting than it does for a small, low-stakes class fundraiser.

Personal Experience.

We've supplied backstop orders for P&C committees who wanted the roster to feel voluntary rather than mandatory. Nobody wants to chase parents for a baking commitment they can't keep. A guaranteed base order takes that pressure off, and volunteers who do want to bake can add to it rather than carry the whole stall.

What Should You Order for a School Fete Cake Stall?

From our experience supplying fundraiser orders, crowd-pleasing flavours consistently outsell adventurous ones at a fete. Chocolate, Vanilla, and Cookies & Cream sell fastest because they're instantly recognisable to a parent walking past with a child in tow.

Unique Insight.

One pattern we've noticed: fete stalls that sell individually wrapped slices move through stock roughly twice as fast as stalls selling whole cakes by the piece. A slice at a fixed, low price point is an easy impulse buy; a whole cake requires a bigger decision and a bigger wallet, which slows the queue and leaves stock unsold by the end of the day.

A Practical Flavour Mix for a Fete Stall.

  • Chocolate and Vanilla — the safest, fastest-selling base of any stall. Order the largest share of your total volume here.
  • Cookies & Cream — reliably popular with school-age kids specifically, not just their parents.
  • Butterscotch or Red Velvet — a mid-tier option for buyers who want something slightly different without going unusual.
  • One distinctive flavour — Lychee or Ferrero Rocher, in small quantity, to give the stall a point of difference worth mentioning.

How Much Cake Do You Need for a School Fundraiser?

A rough planning rule from our fete orders: budget one standard 8-inch cake, cut into 10-12 slices, per 20-25 expected stall visitors. NSW school terms run in four blocks each year, and fetes cluster heavily around NSW Department of Education term end-dates, particularly the run-up to the Term 4 summer break.

Bigger fetes with 500+ expected attendees typically order 25-40 cakes across a mixed flavour range, split between whole cakes for the display table and pre-sliced portions for quick sale. Smaller fundraisers — a single-class cake stall or a Friday morning sale — usually need 5-8 cakes total.

These figures are starting points, not fixed rules. Weather matters more than most committees expect: a hot, sunny fete day pulls more families outdoors and past the cake stall than a cold or wet one, and afternoon slots typically outsell morning slots once the school assembly and main-stage entertainment have wrapped up. If your fete has run before, last year's actual sales numbers are a better guide than any general rule, ours included.

What Other Fundraising Formats Work Well with Cake?

A cake stall is the most common format, but it's not the only one. Cake raffles, "guess the weight" competitions, and class-versus-class cake decorating contests all raise money differently, and each has different supply requirements worth planning for separately.

A cake raffle typically needs one or two show-stopping cakes rather than volume — a single elaborately decorated cake sells more raffle tickets than a table of plain slices. A decorating contest needs blank, undecorated cakes supplied in bulk so each class or family can add their own design. Both formats pair naturally with a bulk order from a catering-focused eggless supplier, since the base cakes need to be consistent even when the decoration varies wildly.

Whichever format your P&C chooses, the planning principle stays the same: decide the format first, then work backwards to the quantity and lead time it actually needs. A celebration-style showpiece cake for a raffle needs far more design lead time than 20 identical slab cakes for a stall.

A minimum of 48 hours for small bulk orders. For a full fete cake stall of 15-30+ cakes, we recommend 7-14 days notice so we can plan production capacity properly and confirm pricing and flavour mix in advance of your event date.

Is bulk cake cheaper than baking for a school fundraiser?

It depends on volunteer time versus budget. Home baking has a lower cash cost but relies on volunteer hours and inconsistent quality. Bulk-ordered cake costs more per unit but frees up volunteers and guarantees consistent, allergy-safe stock for the stall.

Can school cake stalls sell eggless cake as allergy-friendly?

Eggless cake removes egg as an allergen concern specifically, but standard cakes still contain dairy and wheat. P&C volunteers should still label ingredients clearly on the stall table and never claim a cake is fully allergen-free without checking every component first, every single time.

What cake sizes work best for a fete cake stall?

Individually wrapped slices or small 6-inch cakes sell faster at a fete than large whole cakes, since most buyers want a quick, portable treat. We can help plan a size mix based on your expected stall traffic, budget, and price points per item.
