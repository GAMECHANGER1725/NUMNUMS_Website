What Is a Low FODMAP Diet, and Why Does It Matter for Cake?

FODMAP stands for Fermentable Oligosaccharides, Disaccharides, Monosaccharides, and Polyols — short-chain carbohydrates that some people's digestive systems struggle to absorb. In 2025, Monash University marked 20 years since publishing the first low FODMAP diet research, a diet now used worldwide to manage IBS symptoms.

Cake is one of the trickiest foods to navigate on a low FODMAP diet, because its core ingredients — wheat flour, milk, sugar, and fruit — sit across almost every FODMAP category. Removing eggs from the recipe doesn't touch any of that. It solves a completely different problem: eggs are one of eight priority allergens tracked under Food Standards Australia New Zealand (FSANZ) rules, an allergen framework entirely separate from FODMAP sensitivity.

Up to 1 in 10 people globally live with irritable bowel syndrome, and 50-80% of them respond to a low FODMAP diet (Monash University, 20 years of FODMAP research, September 2025). For a condition affecting that many people, clear ingredient information on cake — not just an eggless label — genuinely matters.

What Cake Ingredients Are Highest in FODMAPs?

Wheat flour is the biggest single contributor, thanks to fructans — a type of oligosaccharide wheat is naturally high in. Dairy products like milk powder and butter contribute lactose, a disaccharide. Honey and high-fructose fruits add fructose, a monosaccharide that becomes a FODMAP concern when it outweighs the glucose in the same food.

Not every fruit behaves the same way. Strawberries and blueberries sit relatively low across all four FODMAP categories, which is part of why they're common fillings in low FODMAP baking guides. Mango, apple, and pear carry more excess fructose and tend to be the fruits people on a low FODMAP diet avoid first, ahead of stricter categories like grains or dairy.

Unique Insight.

One detail people often miss: FODMAP content is dose-dependent, not a strict yes-or-no line. A small slice of a moderate-FODMAP cake may sit fine, where the same cake in a large serving triggers symptoms. That's genuinely useful information for anyone managing IBS at a party, where portion control is often more realistic than avoidance.

What Should I Ask a Bakery Before Ordering on a Low FODMAP Diet?

The single most useful question is also the simplest one: "What's actually in this, and how much of it?" A bakery that can't answer specifically, or that just says "it's fine" without detail, hasn't actually engaged with your question. That's true whether you're asking Num Num's or anyone else in Sydney.

A few questions worth asking any bakery before ordering on a low FODMAP diet:

  • What flour is used? Standard wheat flour is high in fructans. A gluten-free alternative substantially reduces that specific risk, though it doesn't address dairy or fruit content.
  • Is dairy involved, and how much? Butter and cream generally carry less lactose than milk powder or condensed milk. Ask specifically rather than accepting a general "contains dairy" answer.
  • What's the sweetener? Cane sugar is low-FODMAP; honey and high-fructose corn syrup are not. This detail rarely appears on a standard ingredient list unless you ask.
  • Can portion size be adjusted? A bakery willing to make a smaller cake, or slice it into smaller-than-usual servings, is giving you a real tool to manage dose, not just a workaround.

Because FODMAP sensitivity varies by individual and by dose, no single ingredient swap guarantees comfort for every person (Monash University, 20 Years of FODMAP, September 2025). Specific, honest ingredient answers from a bakery matter more than a blanket "FODMAP-friendly" label ever could.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is eggless cake automatically low FODMAP?

No. Removing eggs has nothing to do with FODMAP content. Standard cake still contains wheat flour (fructans) and milk-based dairy (lactose), both high-FODMAP ingredients, regardless of whether eggs are used in the recipe or not — ask about specific swaps instead.

Does Num Num's Bakery make certified low FODMAP cakes?

No, we are not a Monash University FODMAP-certified kitchen. We can discuss ingredient swaps like gluten-free flour for wheat-sensitive customers, but we cannot claim any product is verified low FODMAP without that formal certification in place — ask us directly first.

What cake ingredients are highest in FODMAPs?

Wheat flour (fructans), milk powder and butter (lactose), honey, and high-fructose fruits like mango and apple are the main high-FODMAP ingredients in a typical cake. Portion size also matters, since FODMAP load is dose-dependent rather than fixed at a strict cutoff.

Can I request a gluten-free eggless cake from Num Num's?

Yes, gluten-free flour substitution is available on request for custom orders. This addresses the wheat-fructan component of FODMAP content, though the final product is still not certified low FODMAP overall. Message us on WhatsApp before you order to confirm availability and pricing.

How common is IBS in Australia?

Up to 1 in 10 people worldwide live with IBS, according to Monash University, whose researchers developed the original low FODMAP diet. 50-80% of people with IBS respond to a low FODMAP diet, per Monash's 20 years of published research on the topic.
