Eggless Cake vs Cupcakes: Which Should You Order in Sydney?

By Tarun Patel 24 July 2026 10 min read Eggless Cakes

Choosing between a full cake and cupcakes isn't about which one tastes better — at Num Num's Bakery, both are baked from the same 100% eggless batter, in the same 15 flavours. The real decision comes down to your guest count, how the party is structured, and whether the dessert needs to travel. This guide compares eggless cake and eggless cupcakes side by side, so you can pick the format that actually suits your Sydney celebration.

Both formats solve the same underlying problem: a genuinely egg-free dessert that doesn't feel like a compromise. Whether you land on a single showpiece cake or a tower of individually portioned cupcakes, everything at Num Num's — cake or cupcake — comes from a kitchen that has never used eggs at all, so the decision genuinely comes down to logistics and occasion rather than taste or dietary compromise.

What's the Real Difference Between a Cake and Cupcakes for a Party?

The core difference is structural, not a difference in the eggless recipe itself. A cake is one large batter and frosting build, cut into servings on the day. Cupcakes are individually baked and finished, so every serving is already portioned before the party starts. That single distinction drives almost every other trade-off between the two formats — how they're served, how they travel, and how flexible the guest count can be.

From our experience across thousands of Sydney orders, neither format is objectively "better." A milestone 40th birthday with a formal cake-cutting moment calls for something different than a kids' cricket-club presentation night where parents are grazing while children run around. The right choice depends entirely on how your event actually unfolds, not on which dessert photographs better.

The takeaway: if your event has one clear moment where everyone gathers around a dessert, a cake wins. If your event is informal, outdoors, or has guests coming and going, cupcakes are the more forgiving choice.

When Should You Choose Cupcakes Over a Cake?

Cupcakes make the most sense whenever a party is informal or the guest list is uncertain until close to the date. From our order data, school fetes, office morning teas, sports presentation nights and outdoor picnics are the events where cupcakes outsell full cakes by a wide margin.

One pattern we've noticed: cupcake orders spike for events where children under ten are the main guests. Parents consistently tell us it's easier to hand a child one cupcake than to manage a knife, plates and cake crumbs mid-party — a practical reason that has nothing to do with taste or presentation.

We regularly get asked to mix flavours within one cupcake order — say, half Chocolate, half Rasmalai — something that's straightforward for cupcakes but awkward for a single cake. If your guest list has genuinely different tastes across generations, cupcakes let everyone pick without anyone feeling short-changed.

When Does a Full Cake Make More Sense?

A full cake earns its place whenever the event includes a formal moment built around it — candles, singing, a knife, and a photo. Birthdays, engagements, anniversaries and milestone celebrations almost always call for a cake as the centrepiece, even when cupcakes are served alongside it for practicality.

Custom decoration is also a strong reason to choose cake. A single large surface allows for detailed fondant work, photo prints, sculpted toppers and multi-tier builds that simply aren't possible on individual cupcakes. If the design itself is the point — a themed character cake for a child's birthday, or an elegant tiered cake for a milestone event — cake is the only format that delivers it.

How Many Cupcakes or What Cake Size Do I Need for My Guest Count?

Sizing works differently for each format, which is often the deciding factor once the event date is locked in. Cupcakes are ordered in dozens, so you simply round your guest count up to the nearest 12 — 24 for a 20-guest party, 36 for a 30-guest gathering, with a few spare for late arrivals or seconds. A cake is priced and baked by diameter, so the sizing decision has to happen upfront and is harder to adjust once the order is confirmed.

  • Up to 15 guests: A 6-inch round cake or 15–18 cupcakes.
  • 15–25 guests: An 8-inch round cake or 24 cupcakes.
  • 25–40 guests: A 10-inch round cake, or 36–48 cupcakes.
  • 40+ guests: A two-tier cake, or multiple dozens of cupcakes — often combined with a small cutting cake.

Not sure which size fits your event? The Order Online page has a fuller sizing guide, and you're always welcome to check with us directly on WhatsApp — we'd rather confirm the right size before baking than have you left short on the day.

One practical tip from our order desk: if your guest count is genuinely uncertain until close to the date — common with kids' parties where RSVPs trickle in — cupcakes are far easier to adjust at the last minute than a cake size, since we can usually add a few extra to an order right up until the cut-off, whereas a cake's size is locked in once baking begins.

Are Cupcakes or Cakes Better for Outdoor Sydney Events?

Cupcakes generally hold up better outdoors because each one is individually portioned, so heat or an uneven table surface affects one serving at a time rather than an entire build. NSW Health food safety guidance notes that cream and dairy-based fillings need to stay out of the temperature danger zone at outdoor events — a real consideration during a Sydney summer, when a large cake with buttercream or fresh cream can soften quickly in direct sun.

A full cake can still work outdoors with the right handling: keep it in shade, transport it flat, and bring it out only shortly before cutting. But for a picnic, backyard party or all-day outdoor event where the dessert sits out for hours, cupcakes are simply lower-risk and easier to manage without a dedicated cake table.

NSW Health's food safety guidance highlights that dairy-based fillings and frostings need to stay out of the temperature danger zone, particularly at outdoor events (NSW Health, Food Safety). For Sydney's warmer months, that makes individually portioned cupcakes a more forgiving option for all-day outdoor gatherings than a single large cake.

Are Both Options Suitable for Allergies and Dietary Needs?

Both cake and cupcakes at Num Num's come from the same egg-free kitchen, so the allergy and dietary picture is identical whichever format you choose. According to Food Standards Australia New Zealand (FSANZ), egg is one of the eight priority food allergens in Australia, with strict labelling requirements — and cross-contamination from a bakery that also handles egg-based batter elsewhere is a genuine risk for egg-allergic guests.

At Num Num's Bakery, eggs are never used in any product, preparation area or piece of equipment on-site, whether we're baking a three-tier wedding cake or a dozen party cupcakes. That distinction matters for the same range of households, including:

  • Egg-allergic guests — a fully egg-free kitchen removes cross-contamination risk from other egg-based batters entirely.
  • Hindu vegetarian and Jain households — some dietary traditions exclude eggs entirely, whatever the format.
  • Vegan or plant-based guests — note our cakes and cupcakes contain dairy (milk powder, butter); we're not a vegan bakery, but every item is fully egg-free.

If you have specific allergen questions beyond eggs, contact us directly via WhatsApp before ordering, and we'll give you a complete ingredient breakdown for either format.

This consistency is one of the reasons repeat customers stop thinking about the "eggless" part of their order altogether once they've ordered from us once. The decision they're actually making is cake versus cupcakes for their event, not eggless versus regular — every option on the menu already clears that bar. Frequently Asked Questions

Is it cheaper to order eggless cupcakes or an eggless cake?

Per serve, cupcakes and a matching-size cake cost roughly the same at Num Num's Bakery, since both use identical eggless batter and finishing. Cupcakes suit smaller, flexible orders — 12 or 24 at a time — while a cake is priced by size for one large gathering.

Are eggless cupcakes as decorated as eggless cakes?

Yes. Cupcakes take the same buttercream swirls, fondant toppers and themed decoration as a full cake, just distributed across many small servings instead of one large one. Some customers order both — a small cutting cake plus a cupcake tower for guests.

Which travels better in Sydney's summer heat, cake or cupcakes?

Cupcakes generally handle transport and warm weather better because each one is individually portioned and doesn't rely on a single large structure staying level. A full cake needs a flat surface and careful handling, especially with buttercream or fresh cream finishes on a hot day.

Can I order both eggless cake and cupcakes for one event?

Yes. A common combination is a small eggless cutting cake for photos plus a cupcake tower or grazing table for guests to help themselves. Message us on WhatsApp with your guest count and we'll suggest a sizing split for both.
