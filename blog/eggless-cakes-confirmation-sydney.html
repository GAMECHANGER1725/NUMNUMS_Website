How Many Sydney Families Celebrate Confirmation Each Year?

Catholic is Greater Sydney's largest Christian denomination, claimed by 22.1% of the population — 1,154,148 people — at the 2021 Census, ahead of Anglican at 9.2%, according to the Australian Bureau of Statistics. That's over a million Sydneysiders for whom sacraments like Confirmation are recurring family milestones, not once-a-decade events.

Western Sydney carries a disproportionate share of that number. The Diocese of Parramatta serves more than 322,000 Catholics across parishes stretching from Parramatta and Blacktown to the Blue Mountains — the fifth-largest diocese in the country by Catholic population. Confirmation classes run through Catholic primary and secondary schools every single year across that footprint, feeding directly into Term 3 and Term 4 sacrament season.

What Happens at a Confirmation — and Why Does the Sponsor Matter?

A Confirmation sponsor must be a practising Catholic aged 16 or older who has already received Baptism, Communion, and Confirmation themselves, according to the Diocese of Parramatta's Confirmation guidance. The Church recommends the candidate's godparent take on this role, which is why sponsors so often stand beside the confirmand for photos straight after Mass.

Confirmation follows Baptism and First Communion as the third of the Sacraments of Initiation. A bishop or delegated priest anoints the candidate with chrism oil, and many candidates choose a Confirmation name — usually a saint whose life inspires them — though the custom isn't an official requirement of the sacrament. Many Sydney families ask us to pipe that chosen name directly onto the cake.

Among overseas-born Catholics in the Diocese of Parramatta, the Philippines is the single largest birthplace at 8%, ahead of Lebanon at 2.5%, Malta at 1.8%, and India at 1.5%, according to the National Centre for Pastoral Research's 2021 Catholic Census data. Each of these communities brings its own Confirmation reception style to Western Sydney.

Is Confirmation the Same as First Communion?

No — Confirmation and First Communion are two separate sacraments, and in most Sydney parishes they happen years apart. First Holy Communion is typically celebrated around age seven or eight, once a child has completed First Reconciliation, while Confirmation commonly follows later in primary school, according to the Diocese of Parramatta's sacramental guidance.

The two sacraments share a similar cake and reception style — white or ivory tones, family gatherings, a sponsor or godparent standing close by — which is exactly why families sometimes conflate them. If you're planning a First Communion instead, our dedicated First Communion guide covers that earlier milestone in detail, including age ranges and typical guest numbers.
