What Is Rosh Hashanah and Why Do Round Cakes Matter?

Rosh Hashanah commemorates the creation of the world and opens the ten Days of Awe, a period of reflection that ends with Yom Kippur, according to HISTORY's Rosh Hashanah overview. Round challah is traditionally served to symbolise either the cyclical nature of the year or the crown of God, and that same round symbolism carries naturally onto the celebration cake.

Apples dipped in honey are the best-known Rosh Hashanah custom, expressing hope for a sweet year ahead. Honey cake, pomegranates, and other sweet dishes follow the same theme — so a cake built around a naturally sweet, honey-adjacent flavour fits the table without needing to be a literal honey cake.

Round challah at Rosh Hashanah symbolises the cyclical nature of the Jewish year, and apples dipped in honey express hope for sweetness ahead (HISTORY, Rosh Hashanah: Dates, Traditions and History). A round, honey-toned celebration cake extends that same symbolism onto the dessert table.

Is Num Num's Bakery a Kosher-Certified Kitchen?

No — Num Num's Bakery is not a certified kosher kitchen, and we want to be upfront about that before you order. "100% eggless" is a claim about our egg-free preparation process; it is a separate thing entirely from kosher certification, which involves specific supervision, ingredient sourcing, and kitchen procedures we do not currently hold.

If kosher certification is a requirement for your Rosh Hashanah celebration — for a synagogue event, a strictly observant household, or any occasion where certification matters — please order from a certified kosher bakery instead. We'd rather you know that clearly now than find out after ordering.

Why Do Egg-Free Cakes Matter for Rosh Hashanah Guests?

In 2025, egg allergy affects approximately 9% of Australian infants, according to Allergy & Anaphylaxis Australia, and the National Allergy Centre of Excellence's 2025 "Costly Reactions" report found 8.2 million Australians — 30% of the population — now live with allergic disease. Any gathering that spans generations, from young grandchildren to grandparents, is likely to include at least one guest managing an egg allergy or intolerance.

Building a Rosh Hashanah cake around an egg-free bakery removes that worry entirely — no substitution conversation, no cross-contamination question, just a cake that suits the whole table. That matters even more at a holiday meal where the guest list often includes several generations, from young children through to elderly relatives, each with different dietary considerations.

From our order data across Sydney's festival season, multi-generational family gatherings — Rosh Hashanah, Diwali, Lunar New Year, and Christmas alike — are consistently the occasions where customers are most likely to mention an egg allergy or dietary restriction somewhere in the guest list. A naturally egg-free cake sidesteps that entirely.

How Does Rosh Hashanah Fit Alongside Sydney's Other Spring Festivals?

Rosh Hashanah opens a ten-day season of reflection that continues through Yom Kippur and, a few days later, the harvest festival of Sukkot — meaning some households are hosting more than one gathering across a two-to-three-week window in September. That's worth knowing if you're planning catering, because it can be easier to lock in your cake order early rather than competing for a slot in the week everyone else is also ordering.

September also overlaps with the tail end of Sydney's cooler months moving into spring, so fruit-forward flavours like Mango and Pineapple genuinely suit the seasonal shift, not just the symbolic "sweet new year" theme. If you're hosting both a Rosh Hashanah dinner and a Yom Kippur break-fast a week and a half later, consider ordering two smaller cakes in different flavours rather than one large one — it keeps each gathering feeling distinct.

Across the multicultural festival calendar we cater for — Diwali, Lunar New Year, Eid, and Rosh Hashanah alike — spring and early-autumn observances in Sydney tend to draw lighter, fruit-flavoured cake orders. Winter festivals lean towards richer chocolate and butterscotch flavours instead. It's a small pattern, but it's consistent enough that we now expect it every year.

Whichever combination of celebrations your household is hosting, the same 48-hour minimum notice applies, and ordering earlier in the week — rather than the Thursday or Friday right before the holiday — gives you the best chance of your first-choice flavour and pick-up time.
