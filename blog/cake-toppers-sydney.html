Cake Toppers Sydney: A Buyer's Guide

By Tarun Patel 27 July 2026 9 min read Custom Cakes · Birthday

A cake topper is a small piece of decoration doing a lot of work — it's usually the first thing guests photograph, and it's what turns a beautifully finished cake into a specific, personal one. Choosing the right topper, attaching it properly, and knowing when to take it off before serving are three separate skills, and most people only learn the last one the hard way.

At Num Num's Bakery, we build our cakes to leave clean, sturdy space on top for whatever topper you bring — whether that's a name plaque you ordered online, fresh flowers from your florist, or a design we've made for you. This guide covers how to choose, attach, and safely serve a topper, whatever kind you've picked.

What Are the Most Popular Cake Topper Styles in Sydney Right Now?

Minimalist acrylic or gold-mirror script toppers — a name, an age, or a single word like "Congrats" — are currently the most requested style across Sydney celebrations, largely because they photograph cleanly and suit almost any cake design underneath. Fresh floral toppers come a close second, especially for engagement, bridal shower, and anniversary cakes, and fondant figures remain a strong choice for children's parties built around a specific character or theme.

Printed photo toppers — a face or image on edible wafer paper — have also grown in popularity for milestone birthdays and retirement cakes, largely because they're inexpensive and easy to personalise at short notice. The common thread across all of these styles is that Sydney customers increasingly buy or make the topper separately from the cake itself, then add it at the final moment before the party starts.

Australia's cake market is estimated at AUD 1.43 billion in 2025, growing at 3.03% annually toward AUD 1.93 billion by 2035 (Expert Market Research, Australia Cake Market Report). Customisation — including toppers, flavours, and design — is one of the biggest drivers of that growth, as more customers treat the cake as a centrepiece to personalise rather than a standard dessert.

How Do You Choose a Topper That Matches Your Cake Design?

Start with the cake's finish, not the topper's style. A smooth buttercream or fondant cake in a single soft colour works with almost any topper — gold script, fresh flowers, or a bold acrylic shape all read well against a plain background. A cake with heavy texture, piping detail, or a strong colour palette needs a simpler topper so the two elements don't compete for attention.

Size matters more than most people expect. A topper that's too small on a large tiered cake disappears in photos, while an oversized topper on a single-tier cake can look top-heavy and risks tipping. As a rough guide, a topper's width should sit within about two-thirds of the top tier's diameter — wide enough to be the focal point, narrow enough to leave breathing room around it.

Are Non-Edible Cake Toppers Safe to Leave On While Serving?

Only if the topper is made from materials rated food-safe for direct contact with icing — food-grade acrylic, treated wood picks, or edible wafer and rice paper. Craft materials like standard glitter, wire not coated for food use, or glued paper decorations are not food-safe by default, and should be removed before the cake is cut, not left on as a serving-time decoration.

Fresh flowers deserve their own caution. Many common florist flowers are toxic or simply weren't grown food-safe (pesticide residue is the real risk, not the flower species itself), so the general rule from cake decorators and food safety guidance alike is: treat fresh floral toppers as decorative only, remove them before serving, and never let petals or stems touch the icing that will be eaten. If you want genuinely edible flowers, ask your florist or bakery to source pesticide-free, food-grade blooms specifically for the purpose.

How Do You Attach a Topper Without Damaging the Cake?

Timing matters as much as technique. Toppers should go on at the very last step — ideally at the venue, after transport, not before the cake leaves the bakery or your kitchen. A topper pushed in too early can sink further into a softening buttercream finish during the drive, arriving crooked or partially submerged.

  1. Wait until final placement: attach the topper at the venue, right before the cake goes on display, not before transport.
  2. Push straight down, not at an angle: acrylic and wire toppers hold best when inserted vertically into the centre of the top tier.
  3. Reinforce heavier toppers: ask your bakery for a small fondant disc or food-safe pick at the insertion point so weight doesn't pull the topper sideways.
  4. Keep a steady hand and light pressure: too much force can crack a fondant finish or dent a soft buttercream surface.

Should You Buy a Topper Separately or Ask Your Bakery to Include One?

Both approaches work well, and the right choice depends on how specific your vision is. Buying separately — from an Etsy maker, a local Sydney small business, or a craft market — usually offers more design choice and can be cheaper for simple styles, but it does mean coordinating delivery timing so the topper arrives before the cake is picked up.

[Personal Experience]

From our own experience at Num Num's, most customers now bring their own topper rather than asking us to design one from scratch, and we've built our process around that. We leave a clean, level top surface on every custom cake specifically so a separately sourced topper sits properly, and we'll happily talk through sizing over WhatsApp before you commit to buying one.

Asking your bakery to make or source the topper is simpler if you'd rather have one less thing to coordinate, and it guarantees a good fit with the cake's size and finish. Where Sydney Customers Typically Source Toppers.

  • Etsy and online marketplaces — the widest selection of acrylic, wood, and gold-mirror script toppers, usually with a 1–2 week shipping window.
  • Local Sydney small businesses and craft markets — often faster turnaround and the chance to see the material and finish in person first.
  • Your florist — for fresh floral toppers matched to the rest of an event's flowers.
  • Your bakery — for fondant figures, edible photo prints, or a topper designed to match the cake exactly.

Can You Reuse a Cake Topper for Multiple Events?

Yes, and it's one of the easiest ways to get more value from a topper that isn't tied to a specific age or date. Acrylic shapes ("Congrats," a simple heart, an initial), fondant figures without a printed number, and neutral gold script pieces can all move from one celebration to the next without looking recycled, as long as the surrounding design — flowers, colours, cake finish — changes each time.

Age and date-specific toppers are the exception. A "1st Birthday" or a specific number obviously won't suit next year's party, but many families keep these anyway as a keepsake rather than buying disposable versions, since a well-made acrylic or wood topper survives being washed and stored far better than a paper or foil equivalent. If you're buying a topper you'd like to reuse, ask the maker about food-safe, wipe-clean materials specifically — it's a small detail that makes a genuine difference over several years of parties.
