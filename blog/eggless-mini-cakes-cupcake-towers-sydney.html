Eggless Mini Cakes & Cupcake Towers: The Individual-Serve Alternative to One Big Cake

By Tarun Patel 10 August 2026 10 min read Cake Formats · Eggless Cakes

Not every celebration needs one large cake sliced in front of a room full of guests. Across Sydney, eggless mini cakes and cupcake towers have become the go-to alternative for weddings, corporate events, and parties where an individually portioned dessert works better than a single centrepiece. At Num Num's Bakery, every mini cake and every cupcake on a tower is 100% eggless, drawn from the same 15 flavours used across our full-size cakes.

This guide covers what mini cakes and cupcake towers actually are, and when the format beats a single tiered cake. What Are Eggless Mini Cakes and Cupcake Towers?

From the dessert-table orders we take across both Sydney kitchens, mini cakes and cupcake towers are individually portioned versions of the same cake — no slicing, no plating, no cutting ceremony required. A mini cake is a single-serve cake, typically 3 to 4 inches across, iced and finished like a full-size cake but sized for one guest. A cupcake tower is a tiered stand holding anywhere from 24 to 200-plus individually iced eggless cupcakes, arranged so guests simply lift one off as they pass.

Both formats replace the single "cut the cake" moment with a self-serve dessert table. That shift matters for three kinds of Sydney events in particular. At weddings, a mini-cake or cupcake display often sits alongside — or instead of — a traditional tiered cake as a favour-style dessert. At corporate functions, individually portioned cake avoids the need for plates, forks and a designated cake-cutter mid-meeting. And at parties, kids and adults alike can grab exactly the flavour they want without waiting for someone to cut and hand out slices.

The shift also solves a practical problem venues rarely mention: shared-knife cutting isn't always welcome at events with strict food-handling expectations, and a self-serve tower removes that step entirely. Guests pick up their own piece, there's no queue at a cake table, and photographers get a full spread to shoot instead of a single cake mid-slice. For hosts juggling a tight run sheet, that's often the deciding factor over flavour or design.

Should You Choose Mini Cakes or One Big Tiered Cake?

In 2024, NSW recorded 32,050 marriages — more than any other Australian state or territory, and 1.6% higher than the year before (ABS, Marriages and Divorces, Australia, 2024). With that many Sydney weddings each year, the choice between a single tiered cake and an individual-serve dessert table usually comes down to guest count, formality, and how much the cake itself needs to be part of the ceremony.

A single large tiered cake still wins for smaller, more formal guest lists — under about 40 people — where the cutting moment matters and a dedicated cake table makes sense. Once you're catering 60, 100 or 200-plus guests, a mini cake or cupcake tower scales without needing a designated cutting-and-plating window. That matters for corporate functions running a tight schedule and for weddings with back-to-back reception timelines.

How Do You Order Eggless Mini Cakes in Sydney?

Food Standards Australia New Zealand's allergen labelling rules, updated with Plain English Allergen Labelling requirements from February 2024, exist because clarity gets more important, not less, once food is split into many individual servings (FSANZ, Allergen Labelling for Consumers). Made-to-order bakery items work differently to packaged retail food, but the same principle applies at an event: a mini-cake or cupcake tower with 60 individual pieces needs a clear flavour breakdown, not a guess from the host on the day.

Displaying a Cupcake Tower on the Day.

Cupcake towers travel best flat in the box we provide, assembled on-site rather than mid-transit — carrying a fully built tower any real distance risks toppling. We can supply a simple stand for pickup, or you can hire a taller, more elaborate tiered stand from an event supplier. Keep the tower away from direct sun or heat for the first hour after collection, since icing softens quickly outdoors in a Sydney summer. Most hosts assemble it 30-60 minutes before guests arrive, which keeps everything fresh without cupcakes sitting out for hours.

What Do Mini Cakes Cost Compared to a Full Cake?

From our internal pricing data across both kitchens, mini cakes typically cost more per serve than a single tiered cake. Each one is individually baked, iced and finished by hand. A cupcake tower usually lands close to, or slightly under, a comparable single cake's per-serve price. The exact figure always depends on flavour, decoration and order size.

A few factors move the price more than others. Elaborate hand-piped decoration on every mini cake costs more than a simple finish repeated at scale. A taller cupcake tower needs a hired stand, which adds a line item. Rush orders inside the 48-hour window — when we can accommodate them — carry a small premium. None of these apply to every order, so ask for a full breakdown when you request your quote.

[Original Data]

Looking at our own order data from the past year, mini cakes for weddings average noticeably higher per-serve pricing than a same-flavour single tiered cake at 40-plus guests, largely because of the hand-finishing time on each individual piece. Cupcake towers close most of that gap — the per-serve cost drops as tower size grows, since large-batch icing is faster per unit than large-batch full-cake finishing.

As a budgeting rule of thumb: choose mini cakes when presentation and a premium favour-style dessert matter more than cost per head, and choose a cupcake tower when you're catering a large guest list on a tighter per-serve budget. Either way, request a quote through the Order Online page once you know your flavours and guest count — pricing always reflects the specific order, not a flat rate.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the difference between a mini cake and a cupcake?

A mini cake is a small, single-serve cake — usually 3 to 4 inches across — iced and finished like a full-size cake, just scaled down for one guest. A cupcake is baked in its own cup rather than cut down from a larger cake. Both are available 100% eggless in all 15 flavours from Harris Park or Riverstone.

How many mini cakes or cupcakes do I need for my guests?

Plan one mini cake or cupcake per guest, plus a small buffer of 5-10% for late arrivals or seconds. For a 60-guest wedding, that's roughly 65-70 individual pieces. Message +61 425 697 725 on WhatsApp with your exact headcount and we'll confirm quantities before you order.

Can a cupcake tower include multiple flavours?

Yes — mixed-flavour towers are now more common than single-flavour towers for events over 60 guests. Choose two to four flavours from our 15-flavour range and we'll arrange the tower so guests can identify each one. Popular wedding combinations include Ferrero Rocher, Red Velvet and Tiramisu together.

How much notice do you need for a wedding cupcake tower?

Standard orders need 48 hours notice. For a full wedding or corporate tower of 100 or more individually iced cupcakes, we recommend 4-5 days, since every piece is hand-finished. During Diwali, Christmas and peak wedding season, book further ahead where possible.

Are mini cakes and cupcake towers more expensive than a regular cake?

Mini cakes usually cost more per serve than a single tiered cake, since each one is individually baked and iced by hand. Cupcake towers are typically closer to, or slightly under, a comparable cake's per-serve price, especially at larger guest counts. Message us for an exact quote based on your flavour and format.
