What Is a Bat Mitzvah, and How Does It Differ From a Bar Mitzvah?

A Bat Mitzvah marks a Jewish girl's religious coming of age, traditionally observed at 12 — a year earlier than a Bar Mitzvah, which marks the same milestone for boys at 13 (Australian Bureau of Statistics, 2021 Census religion data; NSW Jewish Board of Deputies). Reform and Progressive congregations sometimes mark it at 12 or 13, reflecting genuine variation across Sydney's Jewish communities.

Because age, ceremony structure, and observance vary between Orthodox, Conservative, Reform, and Progressive movements, we won't claim expertise on the religious detail — that's a conversation for a family's own Rabbi or congregation. What doesn't vary from a bakery's point of view is the celebration afterward: a kiddush lunch, an evening party, or often both, each needing a cake sized to the day.

How Many Guests Does a Bat Mitzvah Cake Need to Serve?

From our order history, most Sydney Bat Mitzvah celebrations run to somewhere between 40 and 80-plus guests once the kiddush lunch and evening reception are combined. An 8-inch round covers around 20 guests, a 10-inch about 35, a 12-inch around 50, and a two-tier design comfortably serves 80 or more — sizing that lines up closely with the headcounts most families report when they first message us.

Is Num Num's Bakery Kosher-Certified?

No — Num Num's Bakery does not hold kosher certification of any kind. Being 100% egg-free is a distinct claim from kosher or pareve status, and one does not imply the other. We won't let "eggless" be read as covering something it doesn't.

What the egg-free claim does cover: eggs never enter any product, preparation surface, or piece of equipment anywhere on-site at either location. That removes the egg cross-contamination pathway entirely, which matters for anyone managing an egg allergy in the room. If kosher certification is a requirement for your celebration — for the venue's caterer, a strictly observant guest list, or your own family's practice — confirm the exact certification needed with your Rabbi or a certified kosher caterer before ordering here.

When Should You Order a Bat Mitzvah Cake Relative to the Service Date?

From our order history, families with a Bat Mitzvah booking typically message us several weeks ahead of the event, well beyond our standard 48-hour minimum. That's because the service date is usually fixed 6 to 12 months in advance around the family's assigned Torah portion and the synagogue's calendar. Once it's locked in, there's little flexibility to move it — unlike an ordinary birthday, you generally can't shift the celebration by a week if a supplier falls through.

Do You Need One Cake or Two — For the Kiddush and the Evening Party?

Most Bat Mitzvah celebrations run across two distinct events, and the cake question comes up for both. A synagogue kiddush lunch on the Shabbat morning typically calls for a smaller, simpler cake alongside other sweet treats, while a separate evening party is usually where the larger, more elaborate centrepiece cake appears.

From our order history, families most often order two separate cakes rather than one, sized to match each event's guest count and formality. A modest 8 or 10-inch round suits the kiddush, while a tiered 12-inch or two-tier design anchors the evening party table. If your event only has one gathering, a single well-sized cake covers it easily, and you can always check both pick-up locations to see which fits your morning schedule best.

Are Eggless Cakes Safe for a Guest List With Mixed Dietary Needs?

Eggs sit among the eight priority food allergens tracked by Food Standards Australia New Zealand (FSANZ), with strict labelling laws attached. Separately, Allergy & Anaphylaxis Australia estimates food allergy affects around 1 in 10 Australian infants and roughly 1 in 50 adults. At Num Num's Bakery, eggs never enter any product, preparation area, or piece of equipment on-site, which removes the cross-contamination pathway entirely for guests managing an egg allergy.

Cakes do still contain wheat and dairy, and some designs involve nuts. For guests managing a severe or anaphylactic allergy beyond eggs, we recommend checking with them directly and following your own medical or ASCIA-aligned advice. For full ingredient transparency on any flavour, ask us via WhatsApp before ordering.

At a large event drawing guests from multiple households, it's worth asking the family in advance whether anyone has a specific allergy beyond eggs so we can flag ingredients clearly. A short note on the dessert table listing the flavour and key allergens is a simple, low-effort way to keep the whole celebration genuinely inclusive.
